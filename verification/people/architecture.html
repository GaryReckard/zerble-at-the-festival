Clear, reusable connections.

A population owns who exists, an activity can borrow movement, and a place or vehicle owns its resources. The refactor makes these connections explicit while retaining each family's behavior and rendering.

4 · VisitApproach, participate and exit through compatible commands.
5 · ReleaseRestore ordinary binding and release resources on completion, interruption or unload.

The same visit implementation is proved across two population storage types, two place reservation layouts and two render bindings. A new food-truck service adapter is an extension point; existing truck collision geometry does not imply a service counter.

Where the other people fit

Roles and groups keep the same person

Photographers retain their Crowd identity and pooled camera/flash rendering. Monk groups coordinate existing Crowd members through public intent, avatar and boarding commands. The nut-wagon driver remains the same Crowd person when the wagon releases him. Healing Arts borrows Crowd people and tent-owned rigs.

Vehicle people keep their physics owner

One-wheel riders and Lurleen expose copied person queries without granting activity or boarding authority. Rider collisions remain registry-only. The wagon owns its route, body, seats and music, while Crowd owns its occupants.

Decorative people stay place-owned

Stage performers and Sugar Shack cooks use lightweight display routes; vendor keepers remain tent-owned. They acquire no interactive lifetime merely for animation. Birds remain with their independent ambient flock/perch owner.

Compatibility stays explicit. Children retain gaggle, chase, dodge and smile behavior. Formation members retain group movement, and query registration does not make them eligible for adult venues. New cross-family behavior adds the compatible population policy and resource adapter it actually needs.
