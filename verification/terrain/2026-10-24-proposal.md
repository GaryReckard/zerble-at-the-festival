# A festival with elevation

October 24, 2026. Brainstorm and implementation proposal only. No terrain implementation, browser experiment, benchmark, or gameplay acceptance was performed for this document. Source citations describe the inspected checkout and may move as concurrent work continues.

**TL/DR:** Build a festival in broad rolling country, with level gathering places, contour-following routes, occasional reachable plateaus, and rare mountain districts. Elevation should change what players discover and how they drive. Begin with one shared, deterministic surface definition and a small driving proof, then connect every system to it. Simply raising the ground mesh would leave roads, people, water, passengers, and distant scenery behind.

## What the current code actually establishes

- Earlier sinusoidal terrain was deliberately removed because it clipped paths, lakes, causeways, and decals and appeared to translate with the player (`src/rng.js:77-81`). This makes coherent surface consumers an explicit regression requirement.
- The terrain function is presently a flat-world stub (`src/rng.js:82`), while the followed ground samples it and subtracts the height at the moving mesh center (`src/world.js:198-206`, `src/world.js:531-537`). That subtraction must disappear for persistent world elevations; otherwise the same landmark changes apparent elevation as the ground recenters.
- Ground geometry spans 1,400 meters with 140 segments per side and recenters in 40-meter increments (`src/world.js:28-30`, `src/world.js:168-178`). This is a 10-meter vertex spacing, which is useful for broad hills but cannot faithfully represent a narrow ditch or abrupt campsite grading.
- The pure layout API reserves `groundY` but returns zero, quantizes query coordinates, and documents an append-only layout contract (`src/worldgen/index.js:11-13`, `src/worldgen/index.js:49-76`). Continuous wheel sampling should therefore have its own surface API rather than inherit the one-meter layout quantization.
- Roads already belong to an unordered pair of hearts and are generated end to end, providing a valuable ownership model for a continuous elevation profile (`src/worldgen/roads.js:1-11`). Their rendered ribbon vertices currently have zero height and a constant mesh offset (`src/chunks.js:941-974`).
- Zerble integrates scalar speed, clamps it to forward/reverse limits, translates in X/Z, and forces Y to zero (`src/zerble.js:1248-1280`). Wheel height presently includes decorative oscillation (`src/zerble.js:1292-1303`), and passenger seat conversion accounts for yaw but not chassis pitch or roll (`src/zerble.js:1087-1096`).
- The chase camera already adds cart Y to its position and aim (`src/camera.js:434-448`), but that alone cannot clear an intervening hillside. Collision resolution is explicitly horizontal (`src/main.js:1766`, `src/main.js:1780-1800`), and the registry indexes X/Z (`src/registry.js:72-76`).
- Crowd rendering distinguishes ground-based feet from seat and hammock heights (`src/crowd.js:1640-1665`). Lakes have constant water, island, and beach elevations, and canoe bobbing also uses an absolute flat-world height (`src/lakes.js:512`, `src/lakes.js:530`, `src/lakes.js:568`, `src/lakes.js:846`).
- Far-field descriptors bake local vertical offsets and their road underlay has a constant Y (`src/farField.js:333-409`, `src/farField.js:715`). Both near and distant content must receive the same elevation or their handoff will jump.
- The existing contract preserves RNG streams, chunk/lake ownership, shared resources, four importmaps, and tier behavior (`AGENTS.md:84-111`; `.claude/rules/no-build.md`). Those are constraints on this proposal, not reasons to introduce a new engine.

## Four compelling directions

| Direction | Player experience | Cost and tradeoff |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rolling festival country | A stage disappears behind a low crest, then its lights and crowd reveal themselves as the cart climbs. Gentle valleys separate neighborhoods without walls. | Best first release: meaningful sightline and driving changes with modest slopes, but all grounding consumers still need conversion. |
| Plateau circuit | A broad elevated gathering place overlooks the valley, with a deliberate approach road, a lookout camp, and a memorable downhill return. | Strong navigational landmarks; requires graded approaches and plateau edges that cannot strand the cart. |
| Mountain pilgrimage | Rare districts have a winding ascent, wooded shoulders, a saddle, and a summit gathering. Players choose the long climb because the destination is visible. | Highest route-planning and camera cost. Steep terrain must remain outside ordinary through-routes until traversability is proven. |
| Lake amphitheaters | A level lake sits in a basin while camps and stages occupy terraces above it; shoreline drives make an easy scenic route. | Distinctive festival identity, but water level, shore grading, and all lake-owned content must become one coherent plan. |

**Recommendation:** Combine rolling country with occasional plateau destinations, then add mountain districts using the same surface and route contracts. Use lake basins as a compositional rule from the start so later mountains do not put water on hillsides. These are authored procedural relationships, not independent noise layers competing for space.

Suggested starting values, explicitly tuning hypotheses: ordinary terrain grades of 0–8%, deliberate climbs around 8–15%, broad elevation changes of 10–35 meters over several hundred meters, and rare peaks 60–120 meters above nearby lowlands. Mountain faces may be much steeper, but designated accessible roads must obey the tested cart grade limit. A 15% grade is rise/run, not a 15-degree slope.

Discovery should come from changes in visibility and route choice: a glowing stage glimpsed through a saddle, a quiet camp above a loud valley, a sunrise overlook, a downhill bend revealing a lake. Avoid requiring new collectible systems to justify the geography. A scenic route and a direct graded route can offer a useful choice before any quest exists.

## Proposed surface architecture

Introduce a render-independent, seeded surface service with `sampleSurface(x,z,out)` returning height, normal, grade, surface class, and traversability. Keep a continuous sampler distinct from quantized layout decisions. Specify bounded inputs, independent salts, a terrain version, seed/epoch cache invalidation, and ownership for cached descriptors. Never consume existing chunk RNG as a side effect of sampling.

Use a one-way planning pipeline to avoid a dependency cycle:

1. The independent base relief field supplies broad hills, ridges, and rare mountain candidates.
2. Existing heart, road, lake, and venue descriptors supply constraints. Sample their desired anchor heights from base relief, never from their own final flattened result.
3. Deterministic plans define lake levels and basins, gathering pads, road vertical profiles, and blending shoulders.
4. The final surface evaluates those plans with explicit stable priority and a bounded spatial query. Geometry, driving, placement, and debug visualization consume this result.

Overlapping modifiers require canonical ordering and shared intersection constraints, not first-loaded wins. Define water basin priority and reject incompatible venue/road proposals before rendering. Road/pad junctions share an anchor elevation; independently blending each surface after the fact can create a step at the doorway. Neighbor descriptors must be discoverable before their owning chunk loads, and finite maximum influence bounds must be part of the API.

For the first proof, preserve the existing X/Z plan and shape land around it, allowing cuttings, embankments, and broad graded pads. This isolates vertical integration from layout regeneration. For rare mountain districts, a later explicitly versioned route planner should use contour detours and switchbacks where the old line would require implausible earthworks. Do not quietly alter existing worldgen goldens to make terrain pass.

The render mesh and contact surface must agree. Define a fixed world-space near-ground lattice and its triangle interpolation as authoritative contact geometry, or prove a maximum height error between the analytic field and rendered triangles. A high-resolution physics hill over a coarse flat triangle produces floating tires. Use continuous field derivatives for planning, but derive contact normals from the rendered contact surface. Start with a bounded near patch and coarse outer coverage, measuring whether increased resolution actually needs a more complex ring/LOD design. Different tier meshes must not change physical traversability.

Roads need elevation at both ribbon edges, sufficient subdivision around curvature and grade changes, and a longitudinal profile shared by the full heart pair. Generate the profile before chunk clipping. The same world position must produce the same seam vertex, tangent, and height regardless of which neighbor requests it. Every road-like surface needs the contract, including camp approaches and drum access paths, rather than just arterials.

## Driving that feels grounded

Retain approachable arcade steering, but interpret scalar speed as travel along the local ground tangent. Gravity along that tangent subtracts speed uphill and adds it downhill. With grade angle θ, the gravitational contribution is `-g × sin(θ)` in the forward direction; motor force, rolling resistance, and braking are separate terms. Horizontal displacement then reflects the tangent's horizontal length, preventing slopes from granting extra traveled distance.

The motor's speed limit should limit powered acceleration, while a separate safe coasting limit permits downhill momentum. The present unconditional speed clamp would erase that behavior (`src/zerble.js:1262`). Braking must stop the cart on a supported grade, and a small parking hold prevents annoying rollback when players stop to interact. Beyond motor capability, full throttle should settle or roll back predictably rather than teleport or climb any slope. Tune boost and reverse on the same signed model.

Sample four wheel contacts, derive a support plane, set chassis height/pitch/roll, and use bounded wheel travel for uneven ground. Keep steering yaw independent from chassis tilt and apply animation on a separate transform. Replace decorative vertical wheel oscillation with actual contact response. Preserve the designed cart character with modest body sway rather than making tires visibly detach. Decide explicitly that the initial release remains grounded over crests, with bounded slopes and no jump simulation; an airborne state requires velocity, landing, and collision work beyond this first driving model.

Passenger seats, running boards, lamps, bubble emitters, and carried characters must use full transforms wherever they represent cart-local attachments. In particular, replace yaw-only seat conversion with world matrix conversion. Check every world-space emitter instead of assuming parenting solves it.

Camera behavior should follow a smoothed chassis reference while maintaining a stable horizon, then test the camera-to-target segment against the terrain and move the camera forward/up when obstructed. Sampling only the camera endpoint misses a ridge between it and the player. First-person can follow mild pitch with reduced roll; top-down needs a terrain-relative altitude and a useful maximum view. Teleporting should immediately establish grounding and camera clearance before normal smoothing resumes.

## People, venues, water, and collision

**Gathering places:** Keep stages, food courts, accessible vendor aisles, and seating level on planned pads with graded shoulders. Anchor whole clusters once, retaining local transforms, and publish their world-space interaction and seat positions. Pitching an entire stage to the hillside is not a substitute for grading. Small tents may follow a gentle local plane, but sleeping footprints still need a grade limit and corner-clearance check. Prevent trees from hovering without tilting their trunks to the surface normal.

**Crowd:** Project ordinary walking onto the surface, keep bodies upright, and reduce speed or reject paths beyond a walking grade threshold. Do not overwrite seat/hammock/cart attachment states with terrain height. Roads becoming elevated does not itself teach NPCs to reach a destination across a steep shoulder, so approach corridors must be part of traversability. Preserve existing horizontal broadphase for a single-valued height field, then add vertical overlap checks where needed. Bridges, overhangs, tunnels, and stacked walkable decks are deliberately outside the first surface model because one `(x,z)` cannot describe two floors.

**Lakes:** Give each entire lake one deterministic level and grade its basin and shore against that level. Water remains horizontal. Translate water, islands, beach treatment, canoe motion, campsite anchors, and collider interpretation coherently. For the first release, preserve existing lake outlines and adapt nearby terrain to them. This is intentionally sculpted terrain, not a claim of simulated hydrology. Lake descriptors and colliders must retain their independent lifecycle; a host chunk unloading cannot remove shore protection (`ARCHITECTURE.md:183-190`).

**Distant scenery:** Lift proxies and the road underlay using the same pad/road plans as real geometry. Revisit the player-following decorative mountain ring (`src/world.js:181`): keep it visually subordinate to navigable mountains or replace its silhouette contribution where it would imply a destination that moves away. Terrain may also change what fog and camera clipping reveal, so assess those settings in raised and valley viewpoints rather than copying a flat-world result.

## Phases and the cheapest useful proof

1. **Specify and inspect:** Add a map preview layer showing relief contours, slope bands, graded pads, road maximum grades, water levels, and inaccessible regions. Establish deterministic fixture coordinates and a flat-mode baseline before changing the game. Formal implementation should enter the project's OpenSpec workflow and record the determinism/lifecycle scrutiny decision.
2. **Prove contact and driving:** In the existing 3D sandbox infrastructure, create one bounded terrain fixture with a flat stretch, uphill/downhill pair, crest, cross-slope, plateau pad, and a simulated seam. Use the actual cart update and surface service, with speed/grade/contact-error readouts and replayable input. Keep the fixture small enough to inspect with an orbit camera and a wireframe toggle. This is proposed work, not an existing sandbox feature.
3. **Prove a whole place:** Exercise a real hub builder with a raised level pad, two road approaches, trees, seated/walking NPCs, and a neighboring level lake. Add far-field comparisons and unloading/reloading of the same place. Inspect every registry anchor against rendered placement.
4. **Integrate rolling country:** Wire the live world, all placement families, collision, camera modes, passengers, effects, and lake ownership. Preserve old X/Z layout in this phase and add an independent terrain golden. Complete low/mid/high and real-game checks before enabling terrain by default.
5. **Compose mountain districts:** Add plateau destination placement, intentional steep faces, route feasibility tests, contour roads, and rare summit identities. This is part of the complete proposed destination, but it is gated by proven surface contracts rather than mixed into the first experiment.

The proof should answer three questions before a large rollout: does a hill feel good to drive, can the entire scene share one surface without floating content, and can terrain update within the measured streaming/frame budget? Start with CPU-side mesh sampling and reuse typed arrays and geometry. A worker, shader displacement, or new dependency needs evidence of a bottleneck; none is required merely to express elevation.

No new shadow casters are assumed for terrain. Measure geometry, draws, allocation spikes, surface-query cost, and driving frame-time distributions separately. Cache bounded modifier plans rather than doing full worldgen searches for each wheel every frame. Keep the existing no-build module/importmap loop and shared-resource disposal contract. The proof's browser execution must use the repository's bounded session and teardown rules; no standalone automation is required to write this proposal.

## Proposed acceptance tests

These are future gates, not passed results. Numerical tolerances below are initial engineering targets to validate against the art scale.

| Gate | Required evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Determinism | Identical terrain/pad/road descriptors for a seed across repeated queries and different load/query orders; independent terrain golden; existing X/Z layout goldens preserved in the first integration phase. Test negative coordinates and seed changes. |
| Seams and recentering | Matching edge heights/normals at adjacent ownership boundaries; no physical step above 1 cm; repeatedly cross the 40-meter ground recenter threshold without world height shifts. Cover 80-meter chunk boundaries and modifier overlaps. |
| Mesh/contact agreement | At wheel samples, rendered-triangle and contact heights differ by at most 2 cm on supported ground, including transitions. Visual inspection confirms all four tires and suspension travel remain credible. |
| Driving | Recorded identical throttle inputs on flat, ascent, descent, reverse, and cross-slope fixtures; uphill equilibrium is slower, downhill coasting can exceed powered cruise, braking stops, and parked grade hold stays stable. Repeat at 30/60/120 Hz with bounded final-position and speed error. |
| Terrain limits | An over-limit face cannot be climbed by boost, reverse, diagonal steering, or low-frame-rate steps, and the cart can recover without becoming trapped. Every intended mountain destination has a feasible route. |
| Camera | Chase, first-person, top-down, orbit, and teleport remain above terrain and preserve sight of the player through crests and steep shoulders; no abrupt roll-induced horizon movement. |
| Placement | Stage/vendor/camp corner checks show no burial or floating; seats, interaction radii, passengers, emitters, and headlights agree with their rendered attachment transforms. |
| Crowd | Walking, boarding, disembarking, sitting, hammock use, attraction seeking, and steep-path rejection work on raised terrain without feet snapping to zero. |
| Water | Each lake is planar; shore and islands meet its level; canoe bobbing uses that level; shoreline protection and lake-owned content survive host-chunk unload. |
| Streaming and proxies | Approach/depart/revisit the same terrain across several unload cycles; near/far handoff has no elevation jump, ownership counts return to baseline, and pooled resources remain valid. |
| Performance and compatibility | Measured flat-versus-terrain captures at Noon/Midnight on Low/Mid/High, separate streaming and parked measurements, and real mobile Safari verification. Agree an allowed regression from a fresh baseline before approval; HUD colors alone do not prove success. |
| Release integration | `npm run check`, `bin/check-importmaps`, layout/terrain fixture tests, and a real title-card gesture → mode plank → world boot → drive → console check pass. Documentation, changelog, and remaining roadmap acceptance reflect actual results. |

## Decisions to validate through the proof

The recommendation assumes forgiving hill driving, stable horizons, no airborne jumps in the first release, existing lake outlines, and preserved X/Z layout during vertical integration. The most useful human evaluation is whether a deliberate climb and a rewarding downhill feel playful at festival scale. Exact relief amplitude, grade limits, mountain rarity, and braking strength should follow that drive rather than become untested constants in a large implementation.
