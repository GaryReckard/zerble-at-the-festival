## Architect's Position

### Priority Sequence

1. Characterize current ordinary, hooper, frisbee, and Healing Arts behavior at their existing barriers, then establish one production-backed hula cycle using a Crowd-owned handle. Crowd retains lifetime, personality, ordinary movement, and its collider; the activity owns the hoop, venue reservation, and permitted poses. Current hoopers are independently owned people, so retaining their pool alongside borrowed adults would create additional people instead of a transition (src/people/hoopers.js:21-47; openspec/changes/people-npc-architecture/acceptance.md:18).
2. Add the smallest explicit interruption and resumption seam to the existing claim boundary. When danger or honking applies, revoke activity pose and binding authority before ordinary reaction runs; retain only an activity-owned pending session whose person, venue, reservation and safety are revalidated before acquiring a fresh claim. On failure or timeout, release the reservation and finish. A revoked claim is permanently unwritable and its release callback runs resource cleanup, so treating the original claim as paused is unsupported (src/people/activityClaims.js:24-29,43-46,59-76). If reservation retention is desired, own it separately from the revoked claim and protect it with incarnation checks.
3. Evaluate cart danger for eligible activity participants before the external-claim early return and before any later activity pose writes, reusing the existing direction, reverse, speed, lock and urgency calculations. Preserve the original honk dispatch and its protected-state and exit-clearance rules; apply reaction in the population owner rather than embedding another movement kernel in the prop owner (src/crowd.js:953-954,1112-1129,2504-2549; src/steering.js:73-92).
4. Once the full hula interruption, safe return, and normal departure are proven in the real game, migrate frisbee to a session owning two borrowed handles and one disc. Make acquisition and rollback all-or-none, and make either partner's invalidation interrupt or cancel both. Define held, flying and landed disc transitions explicitly, preventing catcher pursuit or throws during interruption, and validate both lifetimes and the place before a coordinated restart (src/people/frisbees.js:102-125,181-195,228-256).
5. Apply the same interruption policy to eligible Healing Arts dancing while retaining its existing night transition and prior-sample slot, and preserve ordinary stage-front sway as ordinary animation unless a separate reservation is actually required (src/healingArtsActivity.js:113-152; src/crowd.js:963-965; openspec/changes/people-npc-architecture/dependency-slots.md:11-17). Keep eligibility explicit for each population and venue (openspec/changes/people-npc-architecture/extension-guide.md:23-26,139-148).
6. Verify identity and appearance before, during, and after activity, with a single body and collider at every render/contact barrier; exercise partner and place removal, resource replacement, repeated interrupts, and teardown in production-backed tests, then boot the real game. Preserve named frame and scoring barriers and update all five importmaps for any new source module (openspec/changes/people-npc-architecture/dependency-slots.md:8-32; .claude/rules/no-build.md:17-26,39-45; AGENTS.md:29-37).

### Structural Risks Identified

- **Current authority blocks danger detection:** External claims return before Crowd's lane-dodge test, while a late activity can subsequently write the person's position. A mere honk callback or a visual animation cannot make trajectory danger effective; the owner must stop activity writes before a reaction moves the person (src/crowd.js:953-954,1120-1129,2246-2253).
- **Release is destructive, not a pause:** Claim replacement or release invokes its callback and releases owned resources, while `canWrite` rejects the old claim. A resumable reservation needs a separately owned, bounded session and an explicit reacquisition/rollback contract, including person removal and place unload (src/people/activityClaims.js:24-29,43-46,59-76,81-89).
- **Hidden body also suppresses collision and honk:** Crowd currently excludes hidden activity bindings from collider publication and honk processing, and Healing Arts uses that binding for dance rigs. Preserving one visible body and one authoritative collider requires an explicit distinction between a hidden ordinary renderer and a physically protected person, without creating a second collider (src/crowd.js:2256-2267,2402-2411,2522-2524; src/healingArtsActivity.js:48-53,150-152).
- **Appearance fidelity exceeds the existing public view:** Crowd owns full appearance including hair, outfit and accessories, but its public view exposes shirt, skin and scale; Healing Arts copies only shirt and skin. A replacement articulated body that reconstructs from those three fields cannot establish full continuity. Prefer animating the ordinary binding with activity-owned props, or add a deliberately scoped read-only appearance/binding contract and verify all visible attributes (src/crowd.js:658-676,739-747,2158-2163; src/healingArtsActivity.js:48-53).
- **Specialized owners currently publish independent identities and collision sources:** Hula and frisbee both construct person handles and colliders, while frisbee directly moves the catcher. Migration must retire those person publications for borrowed participants and retain only activity-owned props and pair logic; otherwise it duplicates people, queries, or collision authority (src/people/hoopers.js:30-47; src/people/frisbees.js:34-59,228-236).
- **Physical constraints outrank generic scatter:** Existing honk logic exempts riders, and camp exits may fail clearance preflight; bathroom motion has its own hidden and occupied phases. The interrupt protocol must permit a protected or deferred response rather than blindly restoring a walking pose or freeing a resource (src/crowd.js:2120-2148,2430-2433,2522-2545).
- **Frame and lifecycle ownership can drift:** Hoopers and frisbees currently run in `roaming`, after adult scoring, while Healing Arts runs later still. Do not introduce a second population clock or silently shift collection and scoring; venue invalidation and teardown must release borrowed handles before disposing exclusive props, while honoring shared pools (openspec/changes/people-npc-architecture/dependency-slots.md:3-17; src/people/hoopers.js:55-61; AGENTS.md:63-69).

### Verdict

- **Verdict**: Proceed with mitigations
- **Key Concern**: Urgent movement must supersede every activity write without forfeiting lifetime identity or allowing stale claims and reservations to regain authority.
- **Recommendation**: Prove one Crowd-owned hula session end to end, using fresh validated claims on resume and explicit resource ownership, before extending that contract to paired frisbee and dancing.
