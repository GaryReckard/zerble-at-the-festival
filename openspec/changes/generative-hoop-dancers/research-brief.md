# Research brief: convincing hoop dance

Give this brief to two researchers, assigning different emphases:

1. **Technique and movement vocabulary:** what performers do and how moves connect.
2. **Body mechanics and animation:** feet, weight, hips, shoulders, gaze and timing.

## Copyable prompt

Research hoop dance technique for a procedural animated festival performer.

The project is a stylized 3D festival game. We are adding expressive hoop dancers
who compose varied, continuous performances from a small vocabulary of validated
movements. Existing simple waist hoopers will remain.

The central goal is convincing DANCING while manipulating a hoop: footwork,
weight shifts, torso movement, turns, traveling, anticipation, pauses and
recovery. A stationary character performing disconnected arm tricks does not
meet the goal.

Your assigned emphasis is: **[TECHNIQUE AND MOVEMENT VOCABULARY / BODY MECHANICS AND ANIMATION]**.

### Research method

Use primary sources wherever possible: experienced performers, instructors,
tutorials and clearly filmed performances. Watch the relevant demonstrations
rather than relying only on descriptions or search snippets.

Cite specific URLs and video timestamps. Distinguish directly demonstrated or
explicitly taught technique, your interpretation, and your proposed adaptation
for a stylized game character. Do not invent timing, contact mechanics or
anatomical details when a source does not establish them; identify uncertainty.

### Movement vocabulary

Identify approximately 12–18 movements or families covering body hooping and
height changes, hand orbits and overhead work, isolations, passes and plane
changes, supported rolls, tosses/catches, footwork/pivots/travel, and a few
useful two-hoop patterns and partner exchanges. Prioritize recognizable,
adaptable movements over an exhaustive trick catalog. Explain terminology and
name variations between instructors.

### Whole-body mechanics

For each important movement explain which foot supports the weight and how that
changes, what the other foot does, how pelvis/torso/shoulders move relative to
each other, where the performer looks, how arms/hands relate to the hoop, what
prepares the move, and what absorbs or resolves it. Explain what looks wrong if
only the hoop and arms are animated. Describe motion over time, not merely a
final pose.

### Contact and geometry

Identify the hoop plane/path, center movement, hand/body support and contact
changes, spin versus orientation changes, head/torso/limb clearances, and whether
the technique depends on friction, sustained contact or free flight.

Use character-local coordinates: +Y up, +X the character's right, -Z forward.

### Complete phrases

Provide 4–6 coherent 15–30-second phrases with an entrance, recognizable motif,
variation or answer, contrasting pause/held illusion, flourish and resolution
that can lead naturally into another phrase. Explain why each connection works
through matching hands, direction, plane, momentum, stance or musical timing.
Include accessible and elaborate phrases. Use sourced beat counts or explicitly
label proposed choreography. Avoid hand/hoop jumps between unrelated poses.

### Animation recommendations

Identify essential recognition cues, safe simplifications, movements readable
from a normal game-camera distance, distinct performer personalities, safe
variation parameters, constrained relationships, and where stillness or
preparation matters more than constant motion.

We prefer a compact set of excellent reusable movement relationships over a
huge canned animation library. Do not design a general physics engine or assume
motion capture is available.

### Deliverable

Start with a one-page executive summary, followed by a sourced movement table,
detailed whole-body breakdowns, complete phrases, common animation mistakes,
the first five recommended improvements, and an annotated reference library
with direct links and useful timestamps.

Include evidence that challenges our assumptions, especially plausible-looking
animations that are mechanically wrong or visually unconvincing. Quality of
observation and traceable evidence matter more than report length.
