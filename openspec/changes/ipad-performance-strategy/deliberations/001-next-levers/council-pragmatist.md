## Pragmatist's Order of Operations

TL/DR: Stop writing optimizations for a minute. Four shipped changes (Oct 22, Nov 5, Nov 6, Nov 11) have no iPad result, and Gary's iPad time is the scarcest resource. Spend the next device session on ONE capture that carries the Nov 11 build plus the missing per-frame attribution, then pick the next lever from that data. Meanwhile do only desktop-verifiable, low-risk, tail-targeting prep.

### Critical Path

1. **One iPad capture is the gate for everything else.** `ROADMAP.md` (Performance, first bullet) already says to rerun the fixed route and read `ffApply`, `ffApplyFrames`, the far-field world section and time-to-first-horizon. The Nov 8 baseline (`verification/performance/2026-11-08-ipad-child-density/README.md`) is the only comparable "before": 424 ms worst driving interval, 423 ms of it `farField`, 1,421 ms during settling, 25 FPS driving median, ending at `pixel-50`.
2. **Every other candidate depends on what that run shows.** The backlog table (`docs/backlog/performance.md`, "November 11 review: unmeasured candidates") says each candidate "needs an A/B before it is scheduled", and its last row ("Attribute iPad game-update time", about 13 ms per frame outside render and world) is "Measurement only". That row is the force multiplier: it costs no device time if it rides along on the same session as the far-field confirmation.
3. **Why the frame budget matters more than the spikes.** Ordinary driving frames are 22-31 ms total with world about 0-2 ms and CPU render 5-9 ms (briefing digest, Nov 7/8 captures). The 424 ms spikes are felt hitches, but the 25-29 FPS baseline is a per-frame cost problem (about 1,023 draws against Low's 80-draw HUD budget, per the Nov 8 table, and an unexplained roughly 13 ms). Auto is already at the floor (`pixel-50`, 675x462), so fill-rate knobs are exhausted and any further gain must come from CPU/draw/light work. That ordering (hitches fixed, then steady state) is what the evidence supports.

### Priority Sequence

1. **Add the missing iPad attribution to the capture BEFORE Gary runs it (desktop-built, zero device cost).** Extend the existing capture in `src/debug.js` so the same fixed-route report also splits the roughly 13 ms outside render/world (game update: physics step via Rapier, crowd `_updateNpc`, matrix updates, bubbles, HUD) and records draw/program counts per phase. Verify only that the report serializes on desktop (`bin/test-device-capture-flow`-style check, per `.claude/rules/performance.md`). Justification: it converts the single most ambiguous number in the evidence into a named owner, so the next lever is chosen from data rather than guesswork. Rationale for doing it now: re-running the iPad twice is the real cost, not writing a timer.
2. **Gary runs one fixed-route iPad capture on the current `main` build (Nov 11 far-field included), `--autopilot --world-detail --tier auto`**, per `docs/performance-playtests.md`. Read: far-field worst interval vs the 424 ms baseline, time-to-first-horizon, FPS vs 25-29, quality rung reached, and the new game-update split. This single run also covers the Oct 22, Nov 5 and Nov 6 "still awaiting device verification" bullets in `ROADMAP.md`, because they are all in the same build. It cannot attribute each change individually, and I accept that: attribution is expensive, "did the stack help" is cheap.
3. **Branch on the result (decision table, decided in advance so nobody re-deliberates):**
   - Far-field tail gone (worst driving world interval below roughly 50 ms): close the far-field item. Next lever is steady-state frame cost (step 4).
   - Tail persists and is the cold 15-30 ms plan unit: split that unit only (`ROADMAP.md` already scopes it as conditional).
   - Tail is elsewhere (chunk builders at 31-41 ms on device, unloads, lakes): take the matching backlog row (budget unloads / lake deadline) because those are small, code-read mechanisms with a clear surface.
4. **Steady-state lever, ranked by expected draw/CPU yield per risk (all need a device A/B, but the first two are cheap to build):**
   a. **Stop the horizon at the fog limit.** Backlog: over half of kept instances sit beyond 520 m, canopies to 1,508 m, measured at one Low pose. It removes planning cost AND draws, so it attacks both the spike and the steady state. It changes the published set, so Noon/Midnight screenshots on Low are the required gate (existing `hub-sandbox`/far-field viewer plus `/?mute=1`). This is the one candidate that pays twice, which is why it leads.
   b. **Direct render when bloom and Trip are both off.** Backlog (code read, `src/main.js` composer setup): the frame still renders into a half-float target and runs FXAA and `OutputPass` even at Auto's lowest rung, which is exactly where the iPad lived. That is a per-pixel pass chain at an already-small resolution, so expect a modest gain, but it is a config-shaped change. Risk: sky/water/star shader tone-mapping parity must be verified (backlog risk column); I would not ship it without a Low screenshot diff.
   c. **Per-tier context-light pool.** Backlog: 6 point + 6 spot pooled lights plus headlights and the disco spot all day, with Low's per-fragment Lambert. Keep the count constant per tier to avoid recompiles. Only schedule if step 1's split points at fragment/light cost.
5. **Only if steps 2-4 leave Low still far below 30 FPS: the structural levers**, in this order: distant cluster instancing/LOD (draws are the ceiling per the perf-pass-4 backlog entry), then a worker for worldgen. Both are explicitly "evidence-gated" in `docs/backlog/performance.md`; the 2026-11-07 structural-options bundle already declined the broad rewrites, so the bar is a measured device gain, not a design argument.

### Deferred / Cut

-   **Web Workers for worldgen**: the byte-identical probe exists (`.claude/rules/performance.md`, `workerProbe`) but the backlog says it is conditional on transfer, cancellation and async-commit costs, and collider truth must stay synchronous (charter Non-Negotiable list; briefing constraint 5). The far-field fix already turned the biggest stall into bounded pieces on the main thread, so a worker has no confirmed device problem left to solve until step 2 says otherwise. Highest effort, highest tripwire exposure.
-   **Texture / mesh compression (KTX2, Draco, meshopt)**: no evidence of a load-time or memory problem on the iPad; the captures are frame-delivery problems. Backlog itself says "needs its own measured justification" (`docs/backlog/performance.md`, Build tooling).
-   **HUD budget recalibration**: tooling polish. It blocks nothing, and the backlog notes triangles are not the bottleneck. Defer until a device capture gives a number worth recoloring against.
-   **Freeze static chunk subtrees**: about 5% of sampled desktop CPU (backlog row), and the failure mode is silent (animated children stop moving). Poor ratio of gain to subtle-bug risk until step 1 shows matrix updates on the iPad.
-   **Cheaper terrain cache keys**: 19,881 warm lookups took 2.47 ms (backlog), about 1 ms recoverable per cold event; the Nov 6 change already cut this class of cost, and the "no detectable change" frame-delivery verdict in `ROADMAP.md` says more terrain micro-work will not be felt.
-   **Splitting hot chunk builders (Slice 3 / C1-b)**: paused on 2026-07-15 with a clear "if resumed" recipe, but the device chunk peak is 31-41 ms and ordinary world timer is 0-2 ms. It is a real tail, not the dominant cost. Do not resume until the new capture shows chunk generation as the top remaining hitch.
-   **Hidden-tab / audio scheduler resilience (item 7 in `.claude/rules/performance.md`)**: explicitly "not an established cause" of anything. Cut from the perf plan.
-   **More desktop-only micro-benchmarking of anything**: constraint 9 in the briefing says desktop numbers cannot establish a device result; the Nov 5/6 desktop passes both ended "no detectable primary delivered-frame change". Stop here.
-   **Further population/terrain/physics reduction**: protected per briefing constraint 10, and the Nov 8 reduced-child fixture exists but has no device result. I do not propose relaxing any of them. The one exception worth running (it is already built) is the paired `half-v1` iPad run, but only AFTER the main run and only if Gary has device time left; it answers "is crowd/child cost material", which matters if step 1's game-update slice is large.

### Incremental Delivery Plan

-   **Slice 0** (desktop, no iPad needed, ships first): the game-update attribution fields in the capture report, with a regression check that the report still serializes and that default gameplay is unchanged. Safe alone: it is diagnostic output only. Full-game boot smoke (title card, plank click, console) per `AGENTS.md`. CHANGELOG entry (dev-workflow change per `.claude/rules/changelog-and-roadmap.md`).
-   **Slice 1** (needs Gary, one session): fixed-route iPad capture, Nov 11 build. Output is evidence only (a new `verification/performance/2026-11-xx-ipad-...` bundle with a README), then a ROADMAP trim of whichever "Validate/Confirm" bullets the run actually closes.
-   **Slice 2** (desktop-built, device-confirmed in the next session): the horizon fog-limit cull, behind a URL/tier flag so it can be A/B'd on the same device in a single visit (existing `?farField=0` is a precedent for A/B controls, `docs/backlog/performance.md`). Ship default-off until the iPad A/B shows draw or frame gain, then flip. Pilot on the iPad only; production players on Pages are not touched until the flip.
-   **Slice 3** (conditional on Slice 1/2 numbers): direct render when bloom/Trip off, or the context-light pool, whichever the game-update split implicates. One change per device session so attribution stays clean (the "one-variable rule").
-   **Slice 4** (only if still below target): LOD/instancing of distant clusters; worker only if a >50 ms tail survives.

Batching rule for Gary's device time: every iPad session carries a primary question, the standard route, and (if time remains) one A/B toggle already built. Never ship the iPad a build with two untested changes stacked.

### Effort reality check

-   Slice 0 is hours, not days, but the capture serialization is an easy place to break the fixed-route report format that `bin/report-perf-playtest` parses; run that script on an existing capture file before declaring done.
-   The fog-limit cull sounds like a one-line radius filter, but the backlog flags it changes the published set and pool demand on dense seeds. Budget for a demand sweep (the low-tier farField comment in `src/perf.js` already refers to a 5-seed x 24-pose demand sweep, so the tooling pattern exists) plus Noon/Midnight screenshots; this is a day, not an hour.
-   "Direct render" touches the composer path in `src/main.js` and the post-process chain. Tripwire-adjacent (render pipeline / material tier risk signature in the charter), so it needs its own gate; do not treat it as a config flip.

### Verdict

-   **Verdict**: Proceed with mitigations
-   **Key Concern**: Four shipped changes still have no iPad result, and the iPad's 25-29 FPS baseline is dominated by a roughly 13 ms per-frame cost with no device attribution; building any new optimization before one well-instrumented iPad capture risks optimizing the wrong thing with Gary's scarcest resource.
-   **Recommendation**: Land the attribution instrumentation on desktop first (Slice 0), spend one iPad session on the fixed route with the Nov 11 build, then pick from a pre-decided table. If the far-field tail is gone, lead with the horizon fog-limit cull as the single change that cuts both planning and draws. Defer workers, compression, HUD recalibration, chunk-builder splitting and desktop micro-optimizations.

## Round 2 — Reactions

Four of the five Round 1 files (profiler, architect, maverick, adversary, plus mine) converge on the same shape: instrument the untimed slice of the frame, run ONE batched iPad session on the current build, pick the lever from that data. I do not manufacture disagreement with that. The reactions below concern the places where the files actually differ or where a fact needed checking.

### Reactions

1. **Adversary, "Killer Finding": "the median frame interval exceeds the 95th-percentile JavaScript work in every row (32 ms against 22 ms parked)", so the steady frame is mostly outside timed code.** Partly rebut, partly concede. The median-vs-p95 comparison alone does not show a gap: work of 18-22 ms cannot fit in a 16.7 ms vsync, so a CPU-bound frame lands on the next vsync at about 33 ms. That is the Profiler's quantization argument (council-profiler.md, Evidence base item 1) and the Maverick's frame arithmetic (council-maverick.md, step 1), and it fits the 32 ms median. But I checked the retained frame events in `verification/performance/2026-11-08-ipad-child-density/baseline-capture.json`: parked events show `wallMs` 55-66 with `workMs` 18-23 and `previousWorkMs` 16-19. A 20 ms frame would quantize to about 33 ms, not 60 ms, so those tail frames do have a genuine unexplained wait. Caveat: those events are retained slow frames, so the sample is biased toward the worst case and cannot be generalized to the median. Net: the Adversary is right that something outside JS work (GPU process, compositor, power state) matters at the tail; the Profiler and Maverick are right that the median is explainable by quantization. Both readings survive, which is the argument for logging wall-minus-work per phase, not for treating either as established.

2. **Adversary, step 2 and Vulnerabilities: physics (`physicalWorld.advance`, `src/main.js:1288`) is unmeasured, runs outside both timers, and became default after the last fast phone run; add a capture-only `physics=0` arm.** Agree, and sharpen. I confirmed line 1288 in source. This was the item I missed in Round 1: my Slice 0 listed physics only in a parenthetical, and the Profiler also lists it as one of many sub-parts. The Adversary's reasoning that the Oct 22 phone result (57 FPS) predates default physics (2026-10-30 / 2026-11-02 commits) is the only argument in the council that explains why an earlier device run was fast, so it should rank first among suspects to isolate. A `physics=0` arm is a one-flag A/B that costs seconds of device time, which fits my one-variable-per-session rule. I add it to my plan. The Adversary's note that `bin/playtest-perf` cannot append `physics=0` is a concrete task I had not seen; it belongs in Slice 0.

3. **Adversary: serialize `applySuperseded` and horizon age, so a horizon that quietly stops publishing cannot read as a win** (also echoed by the Architect's staleness-window risk and ROADMAP's `ffApplyFrames` read). Agree. This is the cheapest high-value instrumentation item in any file, and it protects the exact claim my plan relies on ("far-field tail gone, close the item"). I had set the pass criterion as "worst driving world interval below ~50 ms"; that criterion is incomplete without the supersession count. Revising it: the far-field item closes only if the interval is low AND the horizon is publishing (low `applySuperseded`, bounded time-to-first-horizon).

4. **Adversary: "Freeze production performance changes until the next iPad run."** Agree in substance, and note I already said "never ship the iPad a build with two untested changes stacked".
