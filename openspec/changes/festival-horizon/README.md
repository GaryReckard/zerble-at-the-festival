## TL;DR

Right now the festival visibly ends a few hundred metres out: the fully built
world stops well before the fog does, so roads and stages vanish into empty
grass and then pop into existence as you drive up. This change adds a cheap
"horizon layer" of simple silhouettes (stage canopies, tent roof peaks, road
ribbons, night lights) drawn in the far distance, using the same deterministic
map data the real world is built from. When the real, fully detailed chunk of
world finishes loading, its silhouette quietly dissolves away. It shipped behind
a `?farField=1` URL flag, passed every visual and performance gate, and was
promoted to **on by default** on 2026-08-28 after Gary's real-device sign-off
(`?farField=0` remains the A/B control).

## The Problem

The game streams the world in 80-metre "chunks" (square tiles of fully built,
interactive festival) around the player. On weaker devices only a 3x3 ring of
chunks is loaded, but the fog doesn't fully hide things until 520m away. The
gap between "last real chunk" and "fog" is empty, so the festival reads as a
small island: destinations don't beckon from a distance, and things pop into
view abruptly. Loading more real chunks would fix the look but multiply the
cost of everything (collision, crowds, audio, shadows), which is exactly what
the weaker devices can't afford.

## Proposed Fix

- Add one new render-only system (`FarField`) that sits beside the chunk
  system, never inside it. It draws a handful of batched silhouettes in the
  middle distance: road ribbons, stage canopies, dark trusses, tent roof
  peaks, and (at night) warm light markers and stage beacons.
- Everything it draws comes from the same deterministic festival "plan" data
  the real builders use, copied into its own buffers, so silhouettes sit
  exactly where the real things will eventually be built. It never touches the
  random-number streams that make the world reproducible.
- It owns nothing gameplay-related: no collision, no crowds, no audio, no real
  lights, no shadows. Hard ceiling of ~6-12 extra draw calls.
- When a real chunk finishes building, the proxy for that spot dissolves out
  over 0.3s (an opaque screen-door dither, so no transparency sorting cost);
  when the chunk unloads again, the proxy comes back. Players with reduced
  motion enabled get an instant swap instead.
- The feature only activates when the modern (v2) world generator is active.
  It shipped dark behind `?farField=1`, passed every measurement gate
  (draw/triangle budgets per device tier, no determinism drift, no leaks,
  clean boot on all tiers), and is on by default since 2026-08-28;
  `?farField=0` is the one-variable A/B control.

## Key Decisions

- **It's a peer, not more chunks.** A separate `FarField` system beside the
  chunk manager; loading more real chunks was rejected as unaffordable.
- **Enablement requires the v2 world.** `?farField=1` alone isn't enough; the
  legacy `?worldgen=0` world gets a zero-cost no-op, because v2 silhouettes
  over a v1 world would never match reality or hand off.
- **Worldgen data is read-only, enforced.** The horizon copies plan data into
  its own records and never mutates the generator's cached arrays; tests hash
  the inputs before and after to prove it.
- **One streaming time budget, chunks first.** The horizon only gets whatever
  is left of the frame's existing streaming budget after real chunk work, and
  it plans incrementally cell-by-cell so a cold query can't stall a frame.
- **Handoff keys off "fully built," by contract.** One shared ownership helper
  plus a narrow `ChunkManager.isLoaded()` predicate, specced to mean "the
  cluster's props exist" so future chunk-loading changes can't silently break it.
- **Promotion is baseline-first.** Disabled-state measurements are captured
  before the gates are finalized; if a device tier is already over its absolute
  budget without the feature, that tier's gate becomes "marginal delta +
  no regression + Gary sign-off" instead of an impossible absolute test.

## Risks & Watch-outs

- **Determinism** (the world must regenerate identically every run): the layer
  consumes existing plan data without new RNG draws, and off/on golden-hash
  comparisons are a hard gate. The sneaky variant is mutating a cached array
  in place, which is why immutability is tested, not assumed.
- **Performance budgets:** it adds draws rather than removing any, so the
  12-draw ceiling, pinned triangle caps (+5k low / +10k mid-high, provisional),
  and resource-plateau checks are promotion gates, verified on `?perf=low`
  and `?perf=mid`, not just high.
- **Stale bounding volumes:** rewriting instanced pools without recomputing
  their bounds makes the horizon vanish at certain camera angles; every
  committed rewrite recomputes bounds by rule.
- **The road underlay** sits at an explicit height between the ground (y=0)
  and the real road (y=0.06), opaque and depth-writing; a module-eval material
  with wrong depth flags has bitten this exact surface before.
- **Deliberately not doing:** bigger chunk rings, replacement LOD for loaded
  content, billboards/textures, forest or lake proxies, new lights or shadows,
  fog changes. Those are separate follow-ups.
