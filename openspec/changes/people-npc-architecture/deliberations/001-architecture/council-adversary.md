## Adversary's Position

### Priority Sequence

1. **Characterize the complete frame before registering controllers.** Preserve the existing legacy slots, but trace their intervening consumers as well as people updates. Smile collection and scoring lie between adult and child updates, while place-driven activity changes happen afterward (`src/main.js:1196-1244`). A people-only trace cannot establish behavioral equivalence.
2. **Specify registration lifetime and claim revocation before implementing handles.** Keep the proposed controller/slot/generation shape only if generation uniqueness survives controller replacement, and invalidate authority before running callbacks that release it. This sharpens the proposed stable-identity and stale-release guarantees (`design.md:85-98`, `specs/people-runtime/spec.md`, “Single lifetime owner and safe identity” and “Explicit movement and activity authority”).
3. **Extract through production-backed adapters and preserve synchronous entry points.** Keep the main tick, existing random streams, render pools, and world ownership intact, then replace private writes with explicit dirty/binding operations. Relevant production boundaries are nested party updates (`src/crowd.js:910-917`), immediate Healing Arts construction (`src/healingArtsActivity.js:6-15`), and chunk-driven release (`src/chunks.js:572-595`).
4. **Consolidate phases only after proving externally observable ordering and repeated teardown.** Verify collisions, smile effects, borrowed rigs, and reservations during two unload/revisit cycles before accepting the final named phases. The plan already requires before/after traces; the corrections below specify what those traces must include (`design.md:104-130`, `tasks.md`, sections 6 and 7).

### Vulnerabilities Found

All findings below are prospective migration hazards, not assertions that the current game exhibits a newly reproduced defect. This was a source and planning review; no browser or device was launched.

- **A clean people schedule can change the smile economy without changing anyone's movement. Severity: High.** Adults update before `smiles.update`, collection, and `Scoring.tick`, whereas children update afterward (`src/main.js:1196-1221`). Children emit directly into the same smile system (`src/obstacles.js:637-664`), whose update ages and moves every active pickup (`src/smiles.js:120-145`). Moving all emissions to the proposed final bounded-effects phase changes which frame ages an adult pickup; moving all people before collection changes that boundary for children. The plan correctly preserves legacy slots initially, but its eventual six-phase target omits these non-person barriers (`design.md:112-124`). **Required correction:** include bubble update, adult emission, collection/coalescing/scoring, child emission, and collision consequences in the dependency trace and characterization fixtures. Keep effects at their original synchronous barriers unless a separately reviewed change intentionally alters them. Assert pickup ages, collection callbacks, score/combo events and order, not only person positions or aggregate smile counts. `git blame -L 1193,1224 src/main.js` traces same-frame collection coalescing to commit `37e58612`; preserve that explicitly documented behavior.

- **Slot generation does not by itself protect controller reincarnation or stale consumers. Severity: High.** The proposed public identity uses `{ controllerKey, slot, generation }`, with generation incremented on pooled-slot reuse (`design.md:87-98`). A removed controller recreated under the same key could restart its counter and reproduce an old handle. Unique simultaneous keys do not prevent this. Place-owned populations and controller disposal are supported by the proposed coverage and cleanup contracts (`design.md`, “Coverage and completion boundary”; `specs/people-runtime/spec.md`, “Idempotent owner cleanup”). **Required correction:** use an opaque registration-lifetime token, runtime-owned generation history, or permanently non-reused controller identities. Choose one small rule rather than adding several identity systems. Test removal, registration under the same logical key, reuse of slot zero, and rejection of the old handle. Include references retained outside people controllers in the inventory: `Smiles.spawnLost` stores a raw NPC record and reads its position later (`src/smiles.js:81-93`, `src/smiles.js:125-128`; caller at `src/crowd.js:1551`). Define whether such effects resolve a live handle or retain a deliberate position snapshot after despawn. A common view must never silently retarget them to a new occupant.

- **A renderer facade can retain stale bindings after a late activity release. Severity: High.** Crowd updates and flags its instance uploads early (`src/crowd.js:951-970`), while Healing Arts updates later in the frame (`src/main.js:1241-1244`) and restores ordinary rendering directly from `releaseNpc` (`src/healingArtsActivity.js:40-50`). The same release can run inside chunk teardown (`src/chunks.js:587-595`). The proposed renderer facade and single visible binding are sound, but a facade that assumes all dirty writes occur during individual movement would miss these paths (`design.md:156-162`; `tasks.md`, Task 4.1, crowd render facade, and Task 4.7, Healing Arts binding). **Required correction:** public bind/unbind/pose commands must dirty every affected instance range independently of whether Crowd updated, and the final upload barrier must follow all permitted same-frame writers. Verify arrival, release, hit interruption, and place unload at that boundary, including a second unload after an ordinary binding has been restored. Assert one visible representation and no stale photographer, hair, accessory, or avatar slots. Preserve shared-resource ownership during cleanup rather than disposing every detached rig (`src/chunks.js:573-582`, `.claude/rules/perf-pooling.md`, “The OTHER pooling pattern”).

- **An explicit actor clock can accidentally become a second game loop. Severity: Medium.** The real loop caps its frame delta at 50ms and gates execution through `shouldRunFrame` (`src/main.js:994-995`); the scheduler uses a 16ms timeout when hidden or in layout-capture mode (`src/main.js:1632-1637`). The plan specifies simulation time and one execution owner, but it should explicitly forbid `PeopleRuntime` from acquiring RAF, timer, or visibility ownership (`design.md:104-130`). **Required correction:** pass the accepted main-loop delta through the runtime; advance its simulation time once per accepted gameplay frame, with no autonomous catch-up. Add visible/hidden/visible and skipped-frame characterization that checks one execution per accepted tick and no extra hidden-tab updates. Preserve the separate audio unlock/resume chain (`src/sound.js:271-350`) and independent world-nightness reads. Clock migration also needs to move comparisons with stored timestamps together: Healing Arts stores `healingCooldownUntil = this.time + 60` and compares it against its supplied absolute `time` (`src/healingArtsActivity.js:45`, `src/healingArtsActivity.js:81-82`, `src/healingArtsActivity.js:110`). Converting just one side silently changes cooldown duration.

### Assessment of the Alternatives

Proceed with a thin runtime and explicit population/activity/render boundaries. The proposed rejection of a full ECS, global event bus, and putting lifetime into the world registry is justified for this migration: existing parties already share Crowd identity, existing visits already substitute a rig, and world cleanup still has separate chunk ownership (`src/monks.js:39-49`, `src/healingArtsActivity.js:21-27`, `src/chunks.js:593-595`). These are real seams to formalize rather than evidence that storage and AI selection both need replacement.

The existing design already addresses the major hard constraints: legacy update order, first-hit collision order, RNG preservation, five importmaps, shared pools, synchronous iOS initialization and real-game verification (`design.md`, “Risks / Trade-offs” and “Verification and extension recipe”; `tasks.md`, opening constraints). Keep those gates. Do not elevate the new runtime into the owner of day/night, vehicle physics, world lifetime or renderer tier selection. The threeShim header explains why material policy must remain at module resolution (`src/threeShim.js:1-20`), and the actual sound initializer retains synchronous resume, a one-sample source and a 100ms WAV (`src/sound.js:271-350`). There is no basis here for changing either path.

### Verdict

- **Verdict**: Proceed with mitigations.
- **Key Concern**: The migration can preserve people-only state while changing frame-level effects, render handoffs, or lifetime resolution across controller replacement.
- **Recommendation**: Incorporate the four explicit acceptance corrections above before implementation. Keep the proposed small coordinator and production adapters, establish complete frame and lifetime invariants first, and make final phase consolidation conditional on those checks rather than an assumed architectural improvement.
