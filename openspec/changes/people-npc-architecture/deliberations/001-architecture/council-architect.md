## Architect's Position

### Priority Sequence

1. **Establish the ownership and execution ledger before extracting modules.** Record lifetime owner, removal-policy owner, movement writer, render owner, resource owner, and exact frame slot separately. Crowd currently performs distant removal, camp activity, party intent, individual updates, and dirty uploads in one method (`src/crowd.js:910-968`), while Healing Arts moves people later in the frame (`src/main.js:1242`, `src/healingArtsActivity.js:124-138`). The inventory and characterization foundation in the proposed tasks is appropriate.
2. **Land the thin runtime and its identity/removal contracts with legacy slots.** Keep existing storage and call order, add generation-safe commands and explicit registration disposal, and prove controller replacement as well as pooled-slot reuse. The proposed register-once shape is a sound integration boundary (`design.md:56-110`); it must not become the owner of chunk, lake, vehicle, or activity resources.
3. **Use children as the first focused population and Healing Arts as the first demanding public person boundary.** Children demonstrate an independent population without the adult state machine (`src/obstacles.js:389-454`), while Healing Arts proves that identity, movement, rendering, and place lifetime can cross ownership boundaries (`src/healingArtsActivity.js:21-50`, `src/healingArtsActivity.js:124-142`). Prove both contracts before repeating the pattern across all activities.
4. **Migrate activities, groups, and render bindings through the public boundaries, then consolidate scheduling.** Preserve the existing monk collective-removal policy through commands to the lifetime owner, and retain efficient instancing. Nested party execution and external activity execution must not be flattened until their dependencies are captured (`src/crowd.js:915-917`, `src/main.js:1193-1244`).
5. **Finish with ordered interaction/query providers, complete adapter cleanup, and an extension proof.** Preserve registry-first first-hit ordering and existing exemptions (`src/main.js:1514-1540`), then verify a new fixture population needs registration and focused services rather than more character-specific orchestration.

### Structural Risks Identified

- **Controller replacement can defeat the proposed handle generations.** This is a prospective gap in the API, not an observed runtime defect. The proposed handle contains only `{ controllerKey, slot, generation }`, and the plan describes increments on pooled-slot reuse (`design.md:87-98`). A disposed controller recreated under the same key could restart its slot generations and accidentally resolve a previous controller's handle. Require a runtime-issued registration incarnation, an opaque identity token, or generations that cannot reset within the resolver's lifetime. Add acceptance for unregistering a controller, registering a fresh controller under its former key, reusing slot zero, and rejecting old commands and releases. The existing specification only gives the within-controller slot-reuse example (`specs/people-runtime/spec.md:17-26`).

- **One lifetime owner does not mean only that owner may decide when removal is requested.** The proposed separation of group membership from lifetime ownership is correct (`design.md:145-154`), but the implementation recipe must preserve the monk policy explicitly. Crowd exempts party members from its independent distance sweep (`src/crowd.js:814-817`), and MonkParty decides collective distance removal before calling Crowd's `removeNpc` (`src/monks.js:115`, `src/monks.js:168-172`). Document that the group supplies a lifetime policy and requests removal through the population owner's validated API; it does not free slots, detach the authoritative binding, or invalidate handles itself. Add collective-distance, partial-spawn rollback, and repeated-party-clear acceptance. Otherwise removing cross-controller writes could accidentally remove the intended group policy or leave cleanup split across two owners.

- **Unload needs an ordered protocol, not only an idempotent final state.** The design requires release on owner unload and shared-resource safety (`design.md:140-150`, `specs/people-runtime/spec.md:83-92`), but it does not define when scheduling and renderer access become invalid. The current chunk teardown traverses and disposes geometry before releasing Healing Arts activity and deleting performer lists (`src/chunks.js:572-597`); activity release can write Crowd matrices (`src/healingArtsActivity.js:40-50`). This establishes an ordering dependency rather than proof of an existing crash. Specify a local teardown sequence: stop participation and new claims, release borrowers while required resource metadata and fallback renderers are valid, detach owned bindings and remove spatial facets, then dispose owned GPU/audio resources. Repeated calls must not reattach a binding or release a later claimant. Add an unload-during-activity test that checks intermediate ordering as well as eventual zero claims. Keep registry add/remove synchronous and nontransactional (`src/registry.js:54-83`, `src/registry.js:87-105`).

- **The renderer boundary must allow read-only world pose without making a model the public person identity.** This distinction in the proposed design is sound (`design.md:85-102`, `design.md:156-162`). Child gameplay currently lives in mesh `userData`, while monks and Healing Arts directly write adult state and invoke private render operations (`src/obstacles.js:418-443`, `src/monks.js:45-49`, `src/healingArtsActivity.js:21-50`). During migration, let an adapter resolve those internals behind commands; do not claim the boundary is complete while external systems still write them. The final dependency rule should keep models as geometry/animation leaves, place builders as lifetime owners, main as the composition root, and the runtime independent of concrete controller imports. Models retain their origin-anchored Group and central-updater contract (`ARCHITECTURE.md:546-552`). A mechanical dependency check can enforce the settled directions, while behavior tests establish actual authority.

- **The eventual phase list is a hypothesis that requires evidence, not a ready replacement for the current frame.** The plan already says this explicitly and should retain that warning (`design.md:104-124`). Vehicles currently move before registry synchronization and Crowd, then smiles and score bookkeeping occur before child and other roaming updates (`src/main.js:1193-1224`). Crowd's nested jobs precede parties and individual decisions (`src/crowd.js:915-950`), while Healing Arts can change final positions afterward (`src/main.js:1242`, `src/healingArtsActivity.js:124-138`). Preserve the legacy slots until a trace establishes which snapshots each consumer requires. In particular, a global render upload at the end must account for late activity movement and collision-driven reactions rather than silently retaining old private flush calls or creating one-frame lag.

### Architectural Fit and Required Amendments

The preferred thin runtime with specialized controllers is structurally stronger than folding everyone into Crowd or adopting a full entity/component storage rewrite. It addresses the actual integration seam without requiring one scene object per adult, changing the registry's role, or erasing distinct movement policies. Existing instance uploads and draw ranges remain within Crowd's renderer facade (`src/crowd.js:953-975`); the registry remains spatial infrastructure (`src/registry.js:1-3`, `src/registry.js:45-53`).

The plan correctly preserves independent world ownership. Chunks, forests, and lakes have distinct load/unload boundaries, and lakes intentionally omit chunk keys (`ARCHITECTURE.md:173-199`). Place-owned performers remain display adapters; their existing chunk tags and teardown remain authoritative (`src/chunks.js:596-597`, `src/chunks.js:3150-3157`). A one-wheel person view must not create another collider or acquire chunk lifetime, because its existing moving registry entry already supplies collision participation and its controller removes that entry (`src/oneWheels.js:63-87`, `src/oneWheels.js:111-123`).

Before accepting this as the implementation contract, amend the design, specification, and tasks with three concrete requirements:

1. Identity remains stale after controller replacement, not only after slot reuse.
2. A group can request collective lifetime decisions while all actual person removal remains with one population owner.
3. Teardown invalidates participation and releases borrowers before disposing resources they need, with explicit intermediate-state acceptance.

The migration must retain the current no-build rule of all five HTML importmaps and the import checker (`.claude/rules/no-build.md:17-40`), pooled-resource exemptions (`src/chunks.js:573-582`), and existing synchronous iOS audio and independent nightness contracts (`AGENTS.md`, constraints 3 and 8). Older two-page and three-page counts in skill/config/charter text are stale relative to the current shared instructions and no-build rule. The submitted plan uses the correct five-page requirement.

### Verdict

- **Verdict**: Proceed with mitigations
- **Key Concern**: The runtime must coordinate participation without acquiring a second lifetime or resource owner, particularly across activity unload and collective group removal.
- **Recommendation**: Retain the proposed architecture and phased migration, strengthen controller-incarnation identity and ordered teardown, and document policy-versus-execution ownership before implementation. These are narrow contract amendments rather than reasons to adopt a larger framework. This review evaluated planning and source only; it makes no runtime or performance verification claim.
