# Independent landscape and travel review

Date: 2026-11-01. Status: design review, not an implementation commitment or a verification report. Reviewer: a fresh Astra sub-agent, requested by Gary. The [main design record](landscape-and-travel.md) preserves the full discussion and reference image. Source line references below were checked against the working tree on this date; concurrent work can move them.

**TL/DR:** Make the landscape generate journeys with recognizable places along them. A bounded mountain district that owns its meadows, banks, access road and sheltered gathering place is the strongest direction here. A bigger noise field or isolated raised venue pads will not deliver that experience reliably. Include a working stream and bridge in the overall delivery, but settle ownership, route feasibility and layered collision before expanding the world. The main unresolved question is how often the generator can produce these complete districts without sacrificing ordinary festival access; neither the current code nor this review measures that rate.

## What Gary has asked for

These are conversation requirements supplied to this reviewer, not facts inferred from the repository or independently established facts about a real property:

- Hills should become noticeable through play, with small through large mountains, steep faces that can be steered around, and occupied areas that are actually reachable. There is explicitly no guaranteed early hill encounter or placement near spawn.
- The mountain camping reference is a sequence: a winding side road, a broad lower camping meadow, a steep grassy bank leading to an upper meadow, then a crest and a short downhill dirt road to a sheltered drum circle and fire. The parent review describes the supplied photograph as showing the meadow, woods and side-road setting; the upper/lower sequence and drum clearing come from Gary's narration. This reviewer did not independently inspect the photograph. This is inspiration, not a request to reproduce a property or its dimensions.
- Streams, rivers and bridges belong in the coordinated landscape discussion. The Barn is a later building with ground access to two levels on different sides, and first aid is a separate future booth or tent request. Neither is an implementation deliverable here.
- Layout positions may intentionally change. Independent deterministic random streams must still remain independent, and loading order must not decide the generated world.

The recommendations below are mine unless identified as one of those requirements. Proposed measurements are acceptance work to perform, not results already obtained.

## 1. The important unit is a district, not a hill

The current generator creates broad relief from an 800 m field with amplitudes bounded by ±15 m, then suppresses it under zero-height venue, camping and lake disks with 90 m shoulders. A 10 m triangle lattice supplies both height and slope. This establishes why increasing raw amplitude alone is a poor first change: the places players visit still erase much of the relief, while the remaining transitions get steeper. See [terrain generation](../../src/worldgen/terrain.js), lines 13–18, 34–51 and 107–139. Live terrain is enabled unless the URL explicitly says otherwise in [main](../../src/main.js), line 178.

**Recommendation:** generate a small vocabulary of complete landforms: a saddle between wooded shoulders, a single knoll with a side approach, and a mountain district containing two irregular meadows and a secluded destination. Give each an outer influence boundary and a finite set of connection ports. The district owns every transition inside that boundary, including the road cut and the steep bank. Nearby venue builders receive supported locations rather than independently demanding contradictory heights.

The best creative idea here is the short descent after the crest. It gives the trip a discovery sequence: climb, briefly see farther, then lose the horizon and enter an intimate clearing. Preserve that relationship while varying orientation, meadow outline, elevation and road length. Do not reduce it to three circular terraces arranged on a straight line, and do not require a drum circle on every mountain. Some mountains should remain natural landmarks, while occupied districts need complete access.

Recognition should come from silhouette, changing sightlines and steering decisions, not only a tilt value. A distant tree line interrupted by a saddle and a road that visibly disappears around the slope could make modest relief more legible. These are visual proposals, not proven effects. Evaluate them from the actual driving camera on Low as well as High, because Low intentionally uses different material and shadow behavior under [the project constraints](../../AGENTS.md), lines 112–116.

## 2. Occupancy geometry is not a foundation specification

Using oriented `clusterShapes` is a useful correction, but treating its entire output as a level foundation would preserve a subtler version of the existing flattening problem. A stage's output explicitly includes its deck circle and the audience clearing rectangle. Vendor rows have a single occupancy rectangle. Those shapes answer where a cluster reserves space; they do not say which portions must be coplanar. See [cluster shapes](../../src/worldgen/tuning.js), lines 270–316.

**Recommendation:** describe at least three distinct needs: level structural supports, gently sloping activity ground, and access clearance. A stage deck needs a reliable foundation; its audience can occupy a bounded gentle slope. A meadow can roll slightly while individual tents, tables or seats use suitable local supports. Do not let a tree clearance or audience reservation acquire the authority to flatten a mountain.

The 10 m lattice makes this more than a shape-library substitution. The current code expands each pad by a triangle diagonal to keep triangles that touch the footprint coplanar, and explicitly clips draped surfaces to the same lattice because endpoint samples alone can leave geometry cutting through the ground. Preserve equivalent geometric guarantees when using smaller oriented foundations. A tighter mathematical footprint can still sit across a large triangle that slopes through it. See [terrain descriptors and draping](../../src/worldgen/terrain.js), lines 41–45 and 151–197.

For isolated raised venues, the previous proposal to reject transition supports that conflict with hard pads is defensible. However, it should not become the mountain district's internal layout algorithm. A district must deliberately share compatible foundations and transitions, or conservative exclusion will discard exactly the clustered camping pattern Gary wants. Outside the district, bounded priority against all competing candidates remains preferable to recursive acceptance searches. Measure both accepted district density and reasons for rejection before calling either policy successful.

## 3. Roads need a feasibility contract before destinations are committed

The current arterial graph is owned by unordered heart pairs, which is a valuable deterministic pattern to preserve. Its neighbors and maximum edge reach are bounded, and lake avoidance works through explicit detours. It does not provide a demonstrated mountain access guarantee. See [road ownership and neighbors](../../src/worldgen/roads.js), lines 1–9 and 23–59, and [lake detours](../../src/worldgen/roads.js), lines 152–196.

**Recommendation:** admit an occupied mountain district only after it has a valid approach from the ordinary road network. Test the whole driven corridor, its width, cross-slope, turns and endpoint joins. A centerline can be gentle while its outside wheel runs on a steep bank. A destination center can be accessible while its tent entrance or turning area is not. In particular, a switchback needs a swept-cart turning envelope and a widened bend that clears both bank and drop. Its inner and outer edges must remain supported through the turn, including the joins between road segments. Test the actual cart controller at a reasonable slow approach speed rather than imposing an unverified generic turning-radius constant; its steering rate depends on speed in [terrain driving](../../src/terrainDriving.js), lines 13–15.

Separate the regional network from district-local circulation. Regional edges connect fixed ports through a bounded corridor; the district owns its winding climb and connections to both meadows. This keeps every ordinary arterial from becoming a search over the complete mountain. Give route search a finite envelope and deterministic candidate order. If no route fits, reject or choose a predeclared alternate district form before populating it. Never emit a festival destination first and hope a road can eventually reach it.

One limitation needs explicit attention: a finite K-nearest-neighbor rule is not, by itself, a proof that the entire infinite world forms one connected graph. For a practical bounded first delivery, require every admitted occupied district to connect to its host network and verify regional component coverage with a halo around the survey. Do not claim a global connectivity theorem from a finite seed survey. The existing bounded neighbor rule is visible in [roads](../../src/worldgen/roads.js), lines 40–54.

## 4. A steep boundary must be recoverable and remain authoritative

The current driving step samples the next point and sets speed to zero when `traversable` is false. The terrain sampler uses total grade ≤0.45, whereas driving force uses grade along the heading. Thus diagonal steering must not silently turn a prohibited face into a climbable one, even if the direction-specific propulsion calculation becomes easier. See [terrain driving](../../src/terrainDriving.js), lines 6–45, and [terrain traversal](../../src/worldgen/terrain.js), lines 137–139.

**Recommendation:** preserve steering and reverse escape at a blocked approach, and allow a bounded slide along the legal boundary where the cart's full footprint remains supported. Treat this as a friendly arcade obstacle, with a visible bank or rock/vegetation cue that corresponds to the actual boundary. Do not add damage just for meeting a steep face unless separately designed.

Movement is not the only operation that changes position. Collision resolution pushes the cart in X/Z after contact, so a valid drive step can still end with an invalid location. The final support check needs to cover collision corrections, spawn, teleport, passenger placement and other relocation paths. The specific correction is in [collision contacts](../../src/collisionContacts.js), lines 48–53, and live contact resolution is called from [main](../../src/main.js), lines 1703–1709. This is a stronger acceptance condition than merely testing forward acceleration against a bank.

Test boosts, reverse approaches, diagonal contact, grazing corners, parking, interrupted frames and a collision pushing toward the edge. A successful recovery must be possible through ordinary controls without debug teleportation. The current 120 Hz substeps and 0.1 s frame clamp are useful existing machinery, but their presence is not proof that those new boundary cases work; see [terrain driving](../../src/terrainDriving.js), lines 7–10.

## 5. Water requires a finite ownership decision, not global hydrology by implication

The river and bridge flags are currently false stubs. The recorded blocker was river avoidance depending on a heart outside the local query window. Merely adding another local avoidance pass would recreate that failure. See [world query](../../src/worldgen/index.js), lines 48–75, and [the river backlog](../../ROADMAP.md#world-generation-procedural-map).

**Recommendation:** begin with finite, region-owned stream systems whose full source, downhill profile, banks, receiving water and crossings are discoverable from a bounded query. One honest complete stream draining into a known receiving basin is more useful than an apparently endless river with hidden discontinuities. Do not infer that arbitrary existing lakes can all be linked downhill: every lake currently has the same zero water level in [terrain](../../src/worldgen/terrain.js), lines 144–146.

If boundary-crossing rivers are essential in the first implementation, their edge ports and water elevations must be fixed before either adjacent region plans its interior. An identifier ordering can prevent cycles but cannot by itself establish downhill continuity or a feasible landscape. Test both. Preserve room for broader drainage later without describing that broader problem as already solved.

The dependency order needs one extra distinction: raw candidates versus committed occupied destinations. Derive raw landform and heart candidates independently, decide bounded landscape/water reservations, establish district ports and route contracts, then place supported destinations and finish local grading. Final grading must obey already accepted water and road envelopes. It cannot create a dam or steep road after route validation. The existing `planning` guard returns zero to avoid recursive final-terrain queries during layout; replace that dependency deliberately rather than working around it. See [terrain planning](../../src/worldgen/terrain.js), lines 58–90 and 107–123, and [independent heart candidates](../../src/worldgen/hearts.js), lines 13–34.

Thin streams also need an explicit geometry decision. The current terrain lattice cannot represent an arbitrarily narrow carved channel faithfully. Keep the coarse landscape, but consider locally refined channel geometry with matching support and contact data, or constrain initial channel widths to what the lattice can actually represent. Rendering a water ribbon over uncut ground is not a completed stream. The shared lattice contract is in [terrain sampling](../../src/worldgen/terrain.js), lines 125–139.

## 6. A bridge exposes more than the height sampler

The most concrete architectural hole is collision. Current contact resolution compares only X/Z distance and radii, without examining height. Merely making the cart stand on a bridge deck would still let it hit objects below. See [collision contacts](../../src/collisionContacts.js), lines 25–29. The spatial registry's X/Z broad phase can stay useful, but candidate contact needs vertical extent and appropriate surface context; see [registry queries](../../src/registry.js), lines 184–186.

**Recommendation:** support selection should take the mover's current surface, elevation and legal transitions into account. Picking the highest surface at X/Z causes teleportation onto a bridge from underneath; picking the lowest causes deck traffic to fall through. Provide stable surface identities and explicit approach connections, while using vertical collision intervals or actual shapes for solids. Do not use differing surface IDs as a blanket collision exemption where a tall object crosses both layers.

NPC grounding also does not establish navigation. For example, the parade advances along a path, applies a dodge offset, then samples terrain height; a camp activity seeks its target and projects around circular solids. Those paths can cross a steep face or leave a bridge unless their reachable corridors and offsets obey the new support rules. See [parade movement](../../src/people/puppetParade.js), lines 129–153, and [camp movement](../../src/people/campActivity.js), lines 283–317.

Water boundaries require coordinated crossing openings. Existing lakes install a sealed collider perimeter with no path gaps in [lake construction](../../src/lakes.js), lines 596–598. A bridge over a similar sealed boundary must either pass above it through valid vertical collision handling or have a crossing-aware boundary model. A visually continuous road is not evidence that the collision ring is passable.

Use the bridge to prove the small layered model needed by the future Barn: connected entrances at different heights, support continuity and height-aware solids. Do not build an interior navigation solver or the Barn itself now. Likewise, do not assume the experimental physics work already solves production terrain contacts; [the physical fixture](../../src/physics/runtime.js), lines 20–21 and 50–66, explicitly separates those integration gates and currently constructs its own fixed ground.

## 7. Delivery gates that demonstrate the intended experience

The following are proposed gates, not completed tests:

| Delivery | What must work before proceeding |
| --- | --- |
| A bounded district proof | One mountain district expresses the lower meadow, steep bank, upper meadow and sheltered descent, with reachable occupied footprints and an ordinary-network approach. Its dimensions are tunable, not claims about the reference property. |
| Deterministic regional planning | The same overlapping region queried in different windows, orders and cache states produces identical committed landforms, routes and crossings. Include deliberately insufficient halos as negative controls. Report admission/rejection distributions and cold planning cost across a seed survey. |
| Recoverable travel | Cart boundaries survive boost, reverse, diagonal approaches, collision corrections and variable frame time. Required NPC activities and visitors can reach their actual locations without crossing forbidden ground. |
| A complete stream and bridge | Water descends to its declared receiver; the channel, banks and crossing agree with rendered geometry. Vehicles, NPCs and representative props use the right support; below-deck objects do not create phantom collisions. |
| Streaming and presentation | Approach, cross, unload and revisit the district from both directions. Contact support must remain present while occupied, without duplicate registrations or stale collision objects. Far-field roads and landmarks preserve the same heights and route silhouette as near-field geometry. |
| Player and device acceptance | Ask a player to describe the landscape and choices after ordinary exploration without announcing the feature. Record encounter opportunities separately from recognition. Measure Low/Mid/High allocation and steady-state costs, including a real phone, before claiming the design is performant. |

Reuse the current pure terrain and mover test surfaces, but intentionally revise obsolete contracts. [The world-terrain test](../../bin/test-world-terrain), lines 11–20, 27, 40 and 55, currently asserts unchanged layout, gentle slopes, one shared datum and a ±15 m envelope. Those assertions should not silently veto the requested design, and deleting them without replacement would lose useful protection. Survey sampling should have explicit spatial resolution and conservative clearance margins; it can miss a thin barrier and cannot substitute for exact corridor checks where those are available. Replace them with versioned-layout expectations, forbidden-versus-accessible slope tests, compatible-support checks and deterministic bounds. Existing negative-control precedent is in [worldgen self-tests](../../src/worldgen/selftest.js), lines 14–21 and 58–82.

For performance, avoid globally reducing the 10 m grid just to support a stream bank. Refine where geometry requires it and measure resampling, geometry creation and uploads separately from parked frame cost. Far-field roads currently sample `terrainHeight` directly, so they need the same road/deck support model rather than an independent approximation; see [far-field road geometry](../../src/farField.js), lines 1013–1035. The project's [performance guidance](../../.claude/rules/performance.md), lines 103–116, distinguishes allocation stalls from steady-state load.

No browser, development server or automated capture was launched for this review. Future automated coverage must first meet the structural teardown requirements in [browser verification](../browser-verification.md), lines 15–26; the prohibited layout capture path is not an available shortcut.

## Review verdict and unresolved measurements

Proceed with a mountain district and a complete stream crossing as the representative design, while using the district boundary to keep ownership and route search finite. The two strongest implementation priorities are separating occupancy from support requirements and making all position-changing consumers respect reachable surfaces and vertical contact. Both are necessary for the requested terrain, and they create the limited reusable foundation that the later Barn actually needs.

The unresolved decisions are the finite river ownership model, the road-search and district influence bounds, and the acceptable district admission/encounter distribution. The unresolved measurements are perceived recognition, route detour cost, rejection density and device performance. The tentative climb heights and runs remain tuning inputs; this source review provides no evidence to promote them into acceptance numbers. None of these open items warrants a guaranteed near-spawn encounter or silently abandoning a complete functional bridge.
